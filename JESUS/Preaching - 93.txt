The true judge of our character


Deuteronomy 4:3 New Living Translation:

> **“You saw for yourself what the LORD did to you at Baal-peor. There the LORD your God destroyed everyone who had worshiped Baal, the god of Peor.**

Back in the Old Testament, baal, was a false god (evil spirit) of many evils. The religious practices surrounding their worship often involved sensuality, ritual prostitution, and child sacrifice to appease the false god to secure a good crop harvest.

What if this evil spirit never actually went anywhere, and people still worship them through their modern societal idols? Things like ruthless capitalism, hyper-materialism, or sacrificing their long-term ethics for short-term prosperity. Anything that takes the place of GOD first and foremost in our lives is feeding this evil spirit instead. Things like our phones and technology taking all our attention and time, the love of money, greed and doing things for a quick buck, but not thinking about the long term consequences. Think about how people in our society give praises to billionaires but mainly go about their own business neglecting the poor and unfortunate.

So, how to avoid this, it all comes back to these simple verses in the Bible:

Matthew 22:37-39 New Living Translation:

> Jesus replied, “‘**You must love the Lord your God with all your heart, all your soul, and all your mind.**’ This is the first and greatest commandment. A second is equally important: ‘**Love your neighbor as yourself.**’

In the context of Jesus' teachings, "your neighbor" is anyone you encounter, regardless of their race, religion, nationality, or social status who is especially someone in need. Imagine if we all did that and helped our neighbors in need, how different would the world be?

Because this is the true judge of our character. At the end of time [we are all judged](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2025%3A31%E2%80%9346&version=NLT). Do you want to hear this said to you?

> “Then the King will turn to those on the left and say, ‘**Away with you, you cursed ones, into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his demons. For I was hungry, and you didn’t feed me. I was thirsty, and you didn’t give me a drink. I was a stranger, and you didn’t invite me into your home. I was naked, and you didn’t give me clothing. I was sick and in prison, and you didn’t visit me.**’ “Then they will reply, ‘Lord, when did we ever see you hungry or thirsty or a stranger or naked or sick or in prison, and not help you?’ “And he will answer, ‘**I tell you the truth, when you refused to help the least of these my brothers and sisters, you were refusing to help me.**’ “And they will go away into eternal punishment, but the righteous will go into eternal life.”
